3. Background
3.1 Why Tokenization Matters
Tokenization is the process of splitting raw text into discrete units called tokens, and mapping
those tokens to integer IDs that the model can process. It sits at the very boundary between the
raw world of text and the numerical world of neural networks.

3.2 Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) Algorithm
BPE is the tokenization algorithm that powers GPT-2, GPT-3, LLaMA, and most modern LLMs.
The algorithm works as follows:
1. Start with a character-level vocabulary. Represent each word as a sequence of characters
with a special end-of-word symbol (e.g., </w>).
2. Count all adjacent symbol pairs across the corpus (e.g., ("l","o") appears many times in
"low", "lower", "lowest").
3. Merge the most frequent pair into a new single symbol: ("l","o") → "lo".
4. Update the corpus representation with the new symbol and repeat from Step 2.
5. Stop when the vocabulary reaches the desired size (e.g., 50,257 for GPT-2).
At inference time, the trained merge rules are applied greedily to tokenize new text.
3.3 Sinusoidal Positional Encoding
The self-attention mechanism in a transformer is permutation-invariant — it does not inherently
know the order of tokens. Positional encoding adds a unique positional signal to each token
embedding before it enters the first layer.
The original Vaswani et al. (2017) paper used fixed sine and cosine functions of different
frequencies:
PE(pos, 2i) = sin( pos / 10000^(2i / d_model) )
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos( pos / 10000^(2i / d_model) )
Where pos is the position index of the token, i is the dimension index, and d_model is the total
embedding dimension (e.g., 512).
